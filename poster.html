Huang returns to Nvidia as board bows to pressure from Trump

The chipmaker's shares jumped 31.5 per cent, adding almost $1tn in value, after an 11-day exile — powering Wall Street's best session since April 2025.

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and chief executive, eleven days after he resigned over revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman US intelligence believed to be a Chinese spy.

The company said the decision followed “input from senior levels of the United States government” and that it was satisfied his return posed no national security risk.

The reversal capped a turbulent fortnight: the board said only last week that Huang would not be coming back, and had begun a search for a successor.

Trump told CNBC he had spoken to the board “directly and indirectly” and that Huang “did nothing wrong”. “I made the deal,” the president said.

Inside the Santa Clara headquarters, staff greeted Huang with chants of his first name across three floors. “Thank you all for the second chances,” he told them.

The pressure had been building for days: Elon Musk, Epic Games chief Tim Sweeney and large investors had all called publicly for his reinstatement.

“I'm going to be a better CEO the second time around.”

Solomon Islands scraps China security pact for Australian treaty

Honiara has told Beijing it will not renew the 2022 agreement, clearing the way for a wide-ranging security deal with Canberra.

Prime Minister Matthew Wale arrived in the Australian capital with a 24-member delegation ahead of Wednesday's signing, which is expected to embed Australia as the country's principal security partner, backed by A$980mn in regional funding through 2028.

Former premier Manasseh Sogavare, now opposition leader, warned the government to “carefully consider what it stands to lose”.

Climate02

COP31 collapses without a deal as fossil-fuel wording sinks talks

The longest UN climate summit on record ended without a cover decision, after two days of overtime in Antalya.

After 44 hours of overtime, negotiators could not agree on language phasing out coal, oil and gas. The EU, Britain, Norway, Panama and Pacific island states refused to endorse a text that did not name fossil fuels.

António Guterres said he was “deeply disappointed” but insisted there was “no ambiguity in the science”. Unresolved items pass to COP32 in Addis Ababa next November.

Fifteen-year-old hits fastest List A hundred off 28 balls

Vaibhav Sooryavanshi's 204 from 53 deliveries, including 28 sixes, carried India A past a target of 250 in Doha.

The Bihar-born left-hander broke Jake Fraser-McGurk's 2023 record of 29 balls, then set a new mark for the fastest double century in the format, reached in 52 deliveries.

India A knocked off the runs in 14.2 overs in the Rising Stars Asia Cup. His 28 sixes are also a List A record.

Euclid spies the most distant quasar yet, 630mn years after the Big Bang

A 360mn-solar-mass black hole at redshift 8.13 breaks the telescope's own record by 40mn years.

The European Space Agency said the object, catalogued J1504+6252 and nicknamed Dún Aonghasa, emerged from 18 months of Euclid survey data. Follow-up observations with the Keck telescopes, Hubble and then the James Webb space telescope confirmed its distance and mass.

Astronomers say a black hole that large, so early in cosmic history, sharpens the puzzle of how such objects formed and fed so quickly. The finding is published in Astronomy & Astrophysics.

The consortium says the object must have grown at close to the physical limit for hundreds of millions of years — a challenge to models of early black-hole formation. Euclid has already found 12 new quasars beyond redshift 7, more than doubling the number known at that distance.

Euclid, launched in 2023, is surveying a third of the sky to map dark energy.
